What Transcribe! is good at

Transcribe! is the analyst's tool. It plays from 5% to 200% in real time, it has a piano-roll view and does note guessing and chord guessing, and it carries markers for sections, measures and beats plus free textual annotations. It has been developed since 1998, it runs on Windows, macOS and Linux, it costs $39 as a one-time purchase, and it works entirely offline with nothing but a licence key.

If you are working a part out by eye and ear — reading a piano roll, naming chords, annotating bar by bar — it is simply better at that than JamCrate is, and it is better at it than most things.

Where it stops fitting

The vendor is explicit that it is not for phones or tablets, so there is no practice-room companion. It has no notation or tablature output — it guesses notes and chords but does not print them — and no way to keep a chart alongside a song. And like every tool of this shape, it opens one file at a time: your library, its folders and its loudness are somebody else's problem.

It is also, deliberately, a dense professional interface. That is a feature when you live in it and a wall when you do not.

What JamCrate does instead

  • Songs as a library: every track gets a folder with its audio, its tab and its markers, and every track is normalised to the same perceived loudness so a song change is not a reach for the volume knob.
  • Tabs and Guitar Pro files shown next to the transport, scrolling with playback — including gp3–gp8 and gpx, which JamCrate opens as a launcher rather than re-drawing as notation.
  • Solo markers and loop chips: mark the solo, and the fragment becomes a loop you can drill without touching the mouse.
  • A phone player with no app store and no second licence: the set opens in the phone's browser over Wi-Fi, or as an offline copy on the device.

What JamCrate does not do, plainly: no note or chord guessing, no piano roll, no annotations, and only 0.5×–1.0× with no speed-up. For transcription work that is a real loss.

Which should you pick?

Pick Transcribe! if transcription is the work: the 5% floor, the piano roll and the note guessing are things JamCrate does not have, and the Linux build has no equivalent here.

Pick JamCrate if you already know the parts and want to play them: a folder of songs that stays in tune with itself, tabs on screen, solo loops under your fingers, and a phone in the practice room.

Questions

Does JamCrate detect chords or notes like Transcribe!?

No. JamCrate has no note or chord guessing and no piano-roll view. It shows the tab or chart you supply and keeps it in sync with playback; working out what is being played is still your ear's job, or Transcribe!'s.

Can JamCrate open Guitar Pro files?

It stores and shows them, and can pair them with the matching audio so they scroll together. It is a launcher, not a notation renderer — for actually editing or printing a gp5 file you still want Guitar Pro or a viewer.

Is there a Linux version of JamCrate?

No, macOS 14+ only. Transcribe! is the one of the two that runs on Linux.

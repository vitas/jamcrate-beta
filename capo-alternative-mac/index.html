A Capo alternative for Mac without a subscription

macOS 14+ · free beta

Capo is the strongest ear-training tool of the group: it detects chords, key and tempo, draws chord diagrams, generates a tab view and can print a lead sheet. Since version 4.0 it is a subscription — Capo Pro at $39.99 a year, Platinum at $49.99 a year on the Mac — and the free edition cannot save your edits. JamCrate takes the opposite position: free, no account, no renewal, a plain folder of files you own. It does not detect chords at all.

What Capo is good at

Capo is the most ambitious of the ear-training tools: it detects chords, key and tempo automatically, draws chord diagrams for guitar, bass, ukulele, mandolin, banjo and piano, generates a tablature view from what it hears, and since version 4.7 can print a PDF lead sheet. It has a Neptune engine that can solo or mute an instrument or vocal, it supports foot-pedal mapping, it syncs projects through iCloud, and it runs on macOS 14+ and iOS 17+.

If what you want is the app to tell you what the chords are, Capo does that and JamCrate does not — that is the honest headline of this page.

Where it stops fitting

Capo moved from a one-time purchase to a subscription at version 4.0, and the free edition cannot save your edits: the current tiers are Capo Pro at $39.99 a year and Capo Platinum at $49.99 a year on the Mac, with $19.99 and $49.99 a year for the iOS version. So a tool that is genuinely good at analysis is rented rather than owned, and the free tier is a demo rather than a product.

Its speed range is 25%–150%, which is narrower than the other tools on this list, and there is no Windows, Android or web version. It also cannot load DRM-protected Apple Music or Spotify streams.

What JamCrate does instead

  • No subscription and no account: you download it, it works, and there is nothing to renew. Every feature is available from the first launch.
  • Your library is a folder of ordinary files, not a project database: audio, tab, markers and notes per song, readable without the app and portable to another Mac or a USB stick.
  • A phone player with no second purchase and no app store — the set opens in the phone's browser, and can be kept offline on the device.
  • Automatic loudness matching across the whole library, so practising through a set does not mean riding the volume.

And what JamCrate does not do: it does not detect chords, key or tempo, it does not generate tablature, it has no lead-sheet export, and its tempo range is 0.5×–1.0×. For learning a song by ear, Capo is the stronger tool.

Which should you pick?

Pick Capo if you are learning songs by ear and want the app to name the chords, draw the diagrams and print a lead sheet — and if a yearly subscription is an acceptable way to pay for that. It is genuinely good at it.

Pick JamCrate if you already know the songs and want to play them: a free library of files you own, tabs on screen, solo loops, matched loudness, and a phone player for the practice room. It will not tell you what the chords are.

Questions

Does JamCrate detect chords like Capo?

No. Capo's chord, key and tempo detection — and the tablature and lead sheets it generates from them — have no equivalent in JamCrate. JamCrate shows the tab, chart or PDF you already have and scrolls it in time with the audio.

Why is Capo a subscription?

The vendor changed Capo to a subscription model at version 4.0 in November 2020. Current tiers on the Mac are Capo Pro at $39.99 a year and Capo Platinum at $49.99 a year, and the free edition cannot save edits. That is the vendor's pricing, quoted from their own App Store listing.

Can JamCrate show a PDF or a chord chart next to the song?

Yes — PDF, txt, chordpro and Guitar Pro files are all stored with the song and shown beside the transport, scrolling with playback. You supply the chart; JamCrate keeps it in the right place and in time.
